Here's the full turn-by-turn of Suspectives (stylized SUSPECTIVES; latest version in The Jackbox Party Pack 11), tracking the main screen and each player's phone separately. Sourced exclusively from jackboxgames.fandom.com and jackbox.wiki.

Setup and lobby
The host launches Suspectives on the shared/main screen. A four-letter room code appears; players join on phones with the code and a display name. Player count: 4–8 (+ Audience). Hosted by The Host (voiced by Dana Quercioli) with Charlie (voiced by Charlie Bickett). Pick a dog detective avatar. Session length is roughly 15–20 minutes.

Phase 1 — secret survey (before any crime)
**Main screen:** Survey intro.
**Player phones:** Answer an **8-question survey** — 7 multiple-choice and 1 fill-in-the-blank — on topics like favorite childhood toy or whether you wore a hat today. Answers are private and become evidence later. Audience answers separately and can vote during investigation.

Phase 2 — crime revealed
**Main screen:** A crime is announced, tied to one survey question. One player is secretly the criminal.
**Player phones:** Check your device — you learn whether you are the criminal. Non-criminals do not know who it is. **One piece of evidence will be phony**, written by a non-criminal player.

Evidence loop (7 pieces, one per remaining survey answer)
Each piece plays out the same way:

**Evidence reveal**
- **Main screen:** One evidence item displays (a survey answer reframed as criminal evidence) plus how many players gave that same answer.
- **Player phones:** See the evidence text. If the count is not everyone (e.g. 2 of 8 picked that answer), all players publicly pin guesses on who the evidence could describe. If **everyone** gave the same answer, no pinning is allowed.

**Interrogation**
- **Main screen:** One player is chosen to interrogate another of their choice.
- **Player phones:** Interrogator asks questions; interrogated player responds. Everyone then votes whether the interrogated player seems innocent or suspicious. Audience votes in parallel.

**Lie Detector (after 3rd and 6th evidence)**
- **Main screen:** Lie detector sequence.
- **Player phones:** Vote which evidence to inspect.
  - If **real:** reveal one player's honest answer to that survey question. If their answer doesn't match the evidence, they are **cleared**.
  - If **phony:** evidence is crossed out; play continues with the next piece.

**Ready to Solve (any time)**
- **Player phones:** Tap **READY TO SOLVE** if you're confident. A **majority** must agree to skip straight to the final verdict — irreversible once triggered.

Final verdict
**Main screen:** Accusation results and guilty/escaped outcome.
**Player phones:**
1. Cast **two votes** for top suspects.
2. Players receiving **zero votes** are eliminated from consideration.
3. Cast **one final vote** accusing the criminal.

Outcomes
- **Tie on final vote → no verdict → criminal escapes.**
- **Majority accuses the real criminal → conviction.**
- **Majority accuses an innocent → criminal escapes.**

End of game
Narrative resolution plays (conviction or escape). No traditional point leaderboard — win/loss is whether the room caught the criminal.

To be straight about the limits: 8-question survey, phony evidence, lie detector timing (after evidence 3 and 6), interrogation flow, Ready to Solve majority gate, and two-vote-then-one-vote finale are documented. Exact number of interrogations per evidence and timer seconds are not published.
