Here's the full turn-by-turn of Word Spud (the version in The Jackbox Party Pack 1 — meta: 2–8 players, ~10 minutes, Writing, no audience, not family-friendly), tracking the main screen and each player's phone separately. Word Spud has no host and no in-game credits sequence. Sourced from jackboxgames.fandom.com and jackbox.wiki only.

Setup and lobby
The host launches Word Spud on the shared/main screen. A four-letter room code appears; players join at jackbox.tv on a phone or tablet with the code and a display name. As each person joins, their name appears in the lobby on the main screen. Two to eight players can join; there is no audience mode. There is no voiced host — the game is UI-driven. Once the room is ready, a round begins.

Round structure and turn count
A full game is one or more rounds; after each round the group can choose to play another, and the game tracks how many rounds each player has won. Within a single round, turns continue until the timer at the top of the main screen runs out (jackbox.wiki). How many turns each player gets per round depends on player count (Fandom):
- 2 players: 11 turns each
- 3 players: 7 turns each
- 4 players: 6 turns each
- 5 players: 5 turns each
- 6 players: 4 turns each
- 7 or 8 players: 3 turns each

A single turn, step by step
Main screen: One player is randomly selected as the active writer. That player is given a starting word or prefix — Fandom examples include simple words like "real" or "life"; jackbox.wiki describes these as prefixes such as "light-" or "super-" that must be turned into a full word or sentence (e.g. "lighthouse" or "super great"). The active word/prefix is shown on the main screen. Non-active players' names/icons are visible; as they type suggestions, those ideas pop up above their icons on the main screen (Fandom).
Phone (active writer): The same prompt appears on the writer's device. They type a fitting or witty word or phrase to follow the prompt, then submit.
Phone (everyone else): While the writer works, other players may type their own ideas on their phones. Those suggestions surface above their icons on the main screen — they are visible to the room but are not the official submission.
The vote (after the writer submits)
Main screen: The writer's entry is displayed for the group. Other players' suggestion bubbles may still be visible from the input phase.
Phone (non-writer players): Each other player votes on the quality of the entry with two options — YES (check) or NO (X). Fandom describes these as a check or an X; jackbox.wiki labels them YES and NO.
Scoring for this turn: Every YES/check is worth +1 point to the writer; every NO/X is worth −1 point (Fandom; jackbox.wiki agrees on add/subtract logic). The writer's net score for the entry is the sum of those votes.

Chain rule — what happens next
If a majority vote YES (jackbox.wiki: majority votes "YES"):
- The writer keeps the points earned.
- The addition becomes the next link in the chain: the last word of a phrase, or the completed word, is passed to the next randomly selected player as their new prompt (Fandom; jackbox.wiki).
- The accepted input appears in the background on the main screen in the writer's color (Fandom).
If the majority votes NO:
- The writer still keeps whatever net points they earned (including negatives).
- The chain breaks: the next randomly selected player receives a fresh random prefix/word instead of the rejected entry (jackbox.wiki; Fandom).

Between turns and end of round
The cycle repeats — new random writer, new or chained prompt, suggestions, vote — until the top-of-screen timer expires (jackbox.wiki). When the timer ends, the round scoreboard appears on the main screen. The player with the most points wins the round and earns a star (jackbox.wiki). Players may then vote to play another round or end the game; round-win counts are tracked across multiple rounds (Fandom).

End of game
After the final chosen round, standings reflect round wins and per-round scores. There is no voiced credits roll with music — Word Spud is the only Party Pack game that does not display credits at the end (Fandom; jackbox.wiki), though credits exist in the game files.

Limits (honest gaps)
The wikis document the timer's existence (top of main screen) but do not publish its exact duration in seconds, nor precise TV-to-phone sync timing. The early Party Pack 1 phone UI is minimal — mostly text entry and YES/NO buttons — so treat any dedicated countdown bar on phones as unconfirmed. Prefix-vs-single-word wording differs slightly between Fandom and jackbox.wiki but the mechanic is the same: one active writer, group vote, chain continues on majority YES.
