Here's the full turn-by-turn of Lie Swatter (the version in The Jackbox Party Pack 1), tracking the main screen and each player's phone separately. A few genuinely frame-level details aren't published anywhere, and I'll flag those honestly at the end rather than invent numbers.
Setup and lobby
The host launches the game on the shared/main screen (TV or stream). A four-letter room code appears, and players go to jackbox.tv on a phone or tablet, type the code and a name. All multiplayer Jackbox games use a smartphone or tablet as the controller via that four-letter code. As each person joins, their name pops onto the lobby on the main screen. Up to 100 players can join, and they can drop in and out at any time, even mid-game. There's no separate "audience" mode here — the player cap already is 100. The fly host (voiced by Phil Ridarelli) then explains the rules out loud before the first round. ebayJackbox Games
A single fact, step by step (Rounds 1 and 2)
Each round is seven facts, and each fact plays out the same way:
Main screen: A cartoon fly buzzes in carrying a category label, and the statement appears "printed on" the fly. Each of the seven facts is shown printed on one of the seven flies. The host reads the category and the statement aloud. The statement is either true or a lie (the wiki's stock example: the claim that 7 Up's name refers to the seven deadly sins, which is a lie). Fandom
Phone: You see the same statement text on your device, with two big buttons — TRUTH and LIE. You're presented with the statement on-screen and on your device, then press TRUTH or LIE. So yes, you read the question on your own phone; you're not forced to look up at the TV. Jackboxpedia
The delay between TV and phone: This isn't an officially published number. In practice the controller updates in near-real-time, so the statement and the buttons become active on your phone essentially as the fact appears on the main screen, give or take ordinary network lag (typically well under a second to a couple of seconds). It is not a long deliberate gap.
The timer: There is a countdown, and it's short. Scoring rewards speed, so the clock is the whole pressure of the game. The official game spec lists "Extended Timers: No," meaning the answer window is fixed and there's no accessibility option to lengthen it. On console, the timer can be paused with the controller's Options button without kicking players out, which confirms it's a real running in-game clock. The most prominent countdown visual is on the main screen; I'm less certain the phone shows a big dedicated timer bar (the early Party Pack controllers were minimal — mostly just the two buttons), so treat "visible timer bar on your phone" as the one thing I can't confirm. Answering: You tap TRUTH or LIE. Your answer time is recorded, because the reward scales with how fast you commit, so you're meant to lock in quickly rather than dawdle.
The reveal (when the timer ends)
Once the window closes, the answer is revealed on the main screen — and the joke is that the bug gets squashed whether you were right or wrong; you only earn points if you were right. The main screen shows whether the statement was TRUTH or a LIE (and typically how the crowd split between the two options). Your phone gives you personal feedback on whether you got it right and the points you earned, so yes — you find out your result right after each fact, not just at the end. Jackbox Games
Scoring: A correct answer is worth 100–150 points in rounds 1 and 2 (200–300 in the final round), and the single fastest correct player gets a 75-point bonus. Earlier correct answers land higher in that band, which is why the timer matters so much. Jackboxpedia
Between facts and end of round
The next fly flies in with the next fact, repeating the loop seven times. After all seven facts, the current rankings (scoreboard) are shown on the main screen. Fandom
Round 3 (final round)
Mechanically identical, with two differences: all seven facts share one single category instead of seven different ones, and points are doubled (200–300 per correct answer). Fandom
End of game
At the end, the top 3 players are shown with the winner in first place, then the full scoreboard is displayed once more so everyone can see where they landed. A full game runs roughly 15–20 minutes. FandomJackbox Games

To be straight about the limits: the structure, scoring, screen content, and the existence of a fixed timer are all well-documented. The two things no public source pins down to the second are (1) the exact delay before the prompt hits your phone — in practice near-simultaneous with the TV — and (2) whether your phone displays a prominent timer bar versus the countdown living mainly on the main screen. If you want those nailed down exactly, the surest way is to watch a playthrough and frame-count, or just launch a quick solo game (it's playable with one player) and watch both screens side by side.
