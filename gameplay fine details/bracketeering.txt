Here's the full turn-by-turn of Bracketeering (the version in The Jackbox Party Pack 4 — meta: 3–16 players plus audience, ~15 minutes, Fill-In-The-Blank, family-friendly), tracking the main screen and each player's phone separately. Host Dandelion is voiced by Phil Ridarelli. Sourced from jackboxgames.fandom.com and jackbox.wiki only.

Setup and lobby
The host launches Bracketeering on the shared/main screen. A four-letter room code appears; players join on phones. Three to sixteen players plus audience — second-highest player cap in the Party Packs after Lie Swatter (Fandom). Dandelion frames the game as strong opinions about stupid things; interstellar bracketeers bet on outcomes (Fandom).

Game structure — three rounds
Round 1: Normal prompt — everyone sees the real question (Fandom example: "Best Grandma Name").
Round 2: First "Blind Bracket" — players answer a decoy/vague prompt that later flips to the real one (Fandom example: write for "Best Kitchen Tool," revealed later as "Best Thing to Kill a Goblin With").
Round 3: Blind Bracket again, but the prompt changes after every bracket matchup ends (Fandom). Points are doubled this round (jackbox.wiki).

Round 1 or 2 — a full bracket pass, step by step
Prompt / write phase
Main screen: Prompt displayed (real in R1; false/vague in R2 until reveal).
Phone: Each player types an answer. If too few players exist or there is an odd count, all answers are used; staff-curated wrong answers credited to "Arn" may fill gaps (Fandom trivia).

Prediction phase (before each matchup group)
Main screen: "Prediction table" — players bet on which answer will win upcoming brackets (Fandom). jackbox.wiki: board shows four answers including your own; pick which will win the matchup.
Phone: Submit prediction for the current four-way group.

First voting layer — four-way groups
Main screen: Four submitted answers shown, separated into groups. Players and audience vote for the best in each group (jackbox.wiki). If time runs out or votes stall, majority wins; on a tie, players tap their chosen answer repeatedly and highest tap count wins the tiebreaker (jackbox.wiki).
Scoring: Players earn points based on vote percentage received in that matchup (jackbox.wiki). Correct prediction → +500 bonus (jackbox.wiki). Fandom describes correct predictions as earning coins — jackbox.wiki uses points; same mechanic.

Semifinal prediction and 2v2 vote
Main screen: Another prediction asks which of the four group winners will take the 2v2 (jackbox.wiki). Voting works the same as above.

Final matchup
Main screen: The two surviving answers face off. Winner earns a 1,000-point bonus (jackbox.wiki). Fandom: player with the most coins/points from brackets plus prediction table wins the game.

Round 3 differences
Blind prompts rotate every matchup (Fandom). All point awards are doubled (jackbox.wiki) — including the 500 prediction bonus and 1,000 finals bonus at double value.

End of game
Main screen: All points tallied; highest total wins (jackbox.wiki; Fandom). Game length roughly 20–25 minutes (Fandom) vs 15 minutes (jackbox.wiki meta).

Scoring summary (jackbox.wiki, primary)
- Match votes → points proportional to vote share
- Correct pre-matchup prediction → +500 (+1000 in round 3)
- Winning the bracket final → +1,000 (+2,000 in round 3)
- Fandom additionally frames prediction-table earnings as "coins" bet alongside bracket performance

Limits
Exact number of four-way groups per round depends on player/answer count and is not fixed in the wikis. Overtime tap tiebreaker duration is unspecified. Fandom's coin language vs jackbox.wiki points may be flavor text for the same currency.
